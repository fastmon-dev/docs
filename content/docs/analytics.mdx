Analytics shows who is on your site, where they come from, which pages and errors they see, and how their visits unfold.

## The dashboard [#the-dashboard]

The main Analytics view for a site shows, from top to bottom:

- **Live line**: the visitors on the site right now. "Watch live" opens a ticker of the newest events: the orders with their value by default, the whole purchase, or every event. Each row opens its visitor.
- **Headline tiles**: Visitors, Pageviews, and Pageviews per visitor, each with a sparkline over the whole range. The change compares against the same weekdays and hours, the fewest whole weeks earlier that cover the window.
- **Sales** beside the chart on a shop: Revenue, Checkout, Orders, and Conversion, each a chart switch with its figure, change, and sparkline. Average order value and revenue per visitor sit beside the title, and "Orders and details" opens the order events, the funnel, and the buyers compared (`?sales=`).
- **Trend chart**: the selected tile or sales row over the range (`?metric=`), revenue per currency. It maximizes alone (`?max=analytics`).
- **Bots** beside the chart type splits the pageviews chart into humans and bots, each bot, or bot type. The tiles and cards keep the page's bot filter.
- **Breakdown**: cards for Pages, Page types, Sources, Locations, Devices, Traffic quality, Campaigns, and Access. A menu picks a card's dimension, the card pages through its rows, and a click on a row filters the page.
- **All values of a card** open from its title in a pop-up with a search, a second dimension, sortable columns, and a CSV download.
- **Visitor paths**: the [checkout funnel](/en/checkout-funnel) and the buyer comparison in one card, visit depth, and visitor lists that open the Visitors page.
- **Active hours**: pageviews by time of day and by weekday as bars. The heatmap is one toggle away.

Line charts draw each bucket at its end. The running bucket of a count, an hour or longer, is dashed towards a projection of the full bucket once a third of it has passed. Release markers are a thin purple line, tracker releases blue.

See [Privacy](/en/privacy) for what a "visitor" means here.

## Shopfloor [#shopfloor]

The Shopfloor, under Analytics at `/shopfloor`, draws your shop from above as it happens.

- **Zones** are your platform's page type groups: Home, Category (PLP), Search, Product (PDP), Checkout, Account, Content, and Other. Each is tinted by how fast its pages are.
- **Every visitor is a dot** in the zone of the page they are on. A tab in the background is drawn faint, and a buyer runs to the exit, where the order counts at once.
- **The window** is 5, 15, 30, or 60 minutes. Under the headline stand today's visitors, orders, revenue, and Conversion Rate from your midnight, each beside the same weekday last week. Orders count up to the second and say so ("as of 14:32"), and last week runs to the same clock time.
- **A click on a zone** zooms into it: its page types, and its top 10 URLs of the last 30 minutes with Pageviews and LCP.
- **For a wall screen**, `?window=60&sound=1&kiosk=1` sets the window, the ka-ching per order, and kiosk mode. Sound is off by default.
- **Filters** are country, device, page type, and traffic. A filter from another page stays as a muted chip marked "not applied here".
- **It needs [`store_stitch`](/en/tracker#store-stitch).** A site without it shows nobody and says so.

The API is [`POST /analytics/live`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsLive) and [`POST /analytics/live-summary`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsLiveSummary).

### Partnerfloor [#partnerfloor]

A partner organization finds the Partnerfloor under **Analytics** in the sidebar. Every live client is one zone.

- **Size and colour**: a zone grows with the client's share of your traffic in the last hour and is coloured by its LCP p75.
- **Each zone** shows its people, cart and checkout, today's orders and revenue, and the change against last week.
- **A click** opens the client's Shopfloor in place.
- **Controls**: the only filter is the traffic class. Window, pause, kiosk mode, and sound work as on the Shopfloor.
- **Live** means an active, approved organization with an activated, active site on an active production application.

The API is `POST /organizations/{org_id}/partner/live`, polled at `meta.next_poll_ms`. It needs direct membership in the partner organization with `partner:read` and `analytics:read`. It takes only `bot_class`, and `data_total`, `client_count`, and `site_count` cover listed clients only.

## Time range [#time-range]

The range pill in the header sets the window for every chart on the page.

- **Zoom**: minus doubles the window, plus halves it, also on the `-` and `+` keys. A live window keeps its end at now.
- **Drag across a trend chart** to zoom into that stretch. The charts reload at the resolution it gets.
- **Jump to now**: while the window lies in the past, a button moves it to the present at the same length.
- **Date and time** are fields that apply at once. The expression field shows the current range, such as `now-7d`, and also takes a past window (`7d-3d`) or a clock range (`14:00-16:30`).
- **The timeline** at the top of the picker shows the window on the days around it. Drag it, pull an edge, or scroll to zoom.
- **Months**: the last 6 or 12 months (keys 9 and 0) for organizations with 13 months of history: fastmon One, Free, Enterprise, and partner clients whose link covers it. Every other organization keeps 90 days, and a longer period from a shared link says so.
- **Complete** (key C) runs from the first pageview of the selected site, application, or organization to now, within that horizon.

### Resolution [#resolution]

The picker and the command palette offer each bucket width with the number of buckets it gives. A width needs 4 to 1500 buckets over the window.

| Width | Fits | On a longer window |
| ----- | ---- | ------------------ |
| Minute | 25 hours | cropped to the last 25 hours |
| 5 minutes | 5 days | cropped to the last 5 days |
| 15 minutes | 15 days | cropped to the last 15 days |
| Hour, 6 hours, day, week, month | 4 to 1500 buckets | not offered |

**Auto** picks the smallest width with at most 300 buckets, and days from 28 days on. The last 24 hours get 5-minute buckets, 48 hours 15-minute buckets, and 7 days hourly ones.

Buckets of 6 hours and longer start on your clock: 6-hour buckets at 00, 06, 12, and 18 local time, a day at midnight, a week on Monday. A day across a clock change is 23 or 25 hours long.

## Filtering

A filter bar sits next to the time picker on every analytics page. Type a dimension and value, for example `country = DE`, and press Enter to add a filter chip. The value field suggests the real values of the time range with their pageviews.

The dimension picker lists Page, Country, and Device first, then browser, OS, connection, viewport, referrer, UTM fields, click-ID label, domain, navigation type, protocol, and the [checkout stage](/en/checkout-funnel). Empty values such as "Direct / none" cannot be filtered yet.

Filters live in the URL and carry across views and shared links. Chips combine with AND, and a comma inside one value means "any of". Click a chip to edit it, its X removes it, and the "Filter" button counts the active ones.

**Every page starts without bot traffic.** The filter menu's Traffic control switches between No bots, Bots only, and All, and the header names the choice next to the period. The scope is not counted as a filter and survives "Clear all".

**Every chip has an on/off switch.** A switched-off chip stays in place, struck through, and neither the query nor the count includes it. Clicking the same country on the map switches it back on, and a shared link keeps the state.

On a phone, a row of chips above the page replaces the filter pill and the side panel, and the time range opens as a bottom sheet.

### Sparse data and time of day [#sparse-data]

**Sparse data** in the **Display** section of the filter pop-up sets apart hours with little traffic. The key `s` switches between Full and Automatic.

| Option | Sets apart |
| ------ | ---------- |
| Full | Nothing. |
| Automatic | The thinnest hours, the same on every card. Strength Low, Medium, or High, and the pop-up shows the threshold and the hour count before you apply. |
| Time of day | The hours outside a range you pick, such as 6 to 23. A range can wrap midnight. |

**Hide** drops these hours from every number on the page and cuts them from the axis of hourly charts, marked by a dotted line labelled "23:00 // 06:00". **Fade** only draws their values pale. Time of day with Hide adds an "Hours" chip to the header, and the live view ignores it.

## The Explorer [#explorer]

The Explorer groups pageviews by any dimension and charts the metrics, or shows the raw rows. Maximize gives it the whole window, sidebar included. "Show all" and the row links of the dashboard panels carry the page's filters into it, and it opens with the page's numbers. Clicking a group opens its pageviews in place, and the bar above steps to the next group or back to all.

The beacon rows show the page's **HTTP status**, and `HTTP status >= 400` in the filter lists the pageviews of error pages. Firefox, Safari, and soft navigations report no status, so their rows show a dash and never match. Grouped by HTTP status, they form the "Not reported" group. The group-by picker has a search field.

An **Events** page sits beside the table, one row per [checkout event](/en/checkout-funnel#stage-events), filterable by type and opening the visitor a row belongs to. A column says whether the row came from a pageview or from the Shopify pixel.

**A pageview opens in a detail with fixed places.** Eight facts and six vital tiles sit at the top, and nine collapsible sections below each show a one-line summary. The field search opens the section that holds a value, for example `ttfb`, and a shared link reopens the same sections.

Results copy as Markdown or download as CSV, with raw values and the unit in the header.

## Navigating with the command palette [#command-palette]

`Cmd`/`Ctrl`+`K` opens a command menu for the whole app: pages, organizations, sites, time range, resolution, and filters. `Enter` or `→` opens a category, `←` or `Backspace` steps back, and `Esc` closes.

Typing searches **every organization you can reach**, your agency's clients included, and their applications and domains. Each hit says which organization it lives in. Applications lead the results, prod before dev among equally good matches, and picking one sets the application filter on the page you are on. Inactive sites and applications are listed and flagged.

Typing a group ("network") lists every page under it, and "API" finds Fetch/XHR.

The same search is available from a script as [`GET /search`](/en/api/search/search).

## Error Analytics [#error-analytics]

Errors sits under Monitoring in the sidebar, with four pages: JavaScript errors, [Failed resources](#failed-resources), [Network Error Logging](/en/nel), and [Content Security Policy](/en/csp). Each has its own address, headline, chart, and list. On JavaScript errors:

- **Affected pageviews over time**: pageviews with at least one JavaScript error, as a share of all pageviews or, under "Absolute", as a count. **Top 5** adds the five errors that reached the most pageviews as lines, and the legend links to each.
- **An error's own page** has the same share and count switch.
- **The list filters by origin**: All, First-party (your own hosts), or Third-party. The choice stays in the link, and the chart and error rate still count every error.
- **The list is one row per fault.** A row counts every variant of a throw site and says how many signatures it stands for. On the API this is `group_variants` on `POST /analytics/error-types`.
- **A row leads with what broke, in a sentence**: what happened, whose code it is, the file, how often, and when it last occurred. Each row carries a spark line and a "New" or "Rising" badge, and the list sorts by most frequent, new, rising, or last seen.
- **Ranked by the pageviews an error reached.** A visit stuck in a loop is marked as a loop, and "Most frequent" stays an ordering you can pick.
- **Every error has its own page** at `/org/<org>/errors/<fingerprint>`, which is also its share link. Existing `?error=` links redirect there.
- **Pages with the most errors**: ranked by how many errors a page threw, not by its error rate.
- **Error origin**: how pageviews with an error split between your own hosts and foreign ones. The third bar holds pageviews without an error plus errors with no host to attribute them to.

### What broke, and where [#error-detail]

**An error says what broke in a sentence**, even where the site keeps no error messages. The tracker recognizes the browser's wording and sends its [shape](/en/tracker#message-shape) rather than the text, so a row reads "`uc.setServiceAlias` was called, but `uc` does not exist at this point" instead of showing a stack alone.

**"Resolve this stack" reads the minified file your visitors actually ran.** Each frame comes back with its method, its module, and the code at that column, the throwing line marked. It needs no source map.

- A run of frames in the same vendor bundle folds into one row naming the library. When every frame is a library, the panel says so: the call that made the mistake was cut off at five frames.
- A frame that only passes the error on is marked as a rethrow, and the cause sits further down.
- A frame that cannot be read says why: no path was stored, the file sits on somebody else's host, the file is the fastmon tracker, or you deployed again under the same name and the position is gone.
- The endpoint is [`POST /analytics/error-types/symbolicate`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsErrorSymbolication). The same panel sits in the Explorer's row drawer: open a pageview, expand its errors, and the stack resolves there too.

**"Where it happens" is the breakdown grid the metric pages carry**: page, page type, domain, country, device, browser, OS, connection, navigation, caches, bots, and network.

**An error without message or stack says why**: messages or frames switched off, a third-party script without the CORS opt-in, or nothing sent. The chart and the breakdown work either way.

**"AI prompt" hands the whole case to an assistant**: what broke, the numbers, the resolved stack with the code at the throw site, where it happens, and sample occurrences. It also says what your site does not collect.

**Which API calls fail alongside it.** [`POST /analytics/error-types/correlated-requests`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsErrorCorrelatedRequests) ranks the fetch/XHR endpoints that fail in the same pageviews, each against its failure share in pageviews without that error.

**An error signature is a filter like any other.** Every error row links into the Explorer scoped to the pageviews that carried it, and "Error signature" is in the filter list. `filters.error_fingerprint_kind` says which list the key came from (`error` or `failed_resource`).

**Your own subdomains count as yours.** First party means the same registrable domain as the page, in the error rate, the error origin, the Fetch/XHR split, and the resource list alike.

`ScriptError` is an error in a cross-origin script loaded without CORS, whose details the browser hides. Load it with `crossorigin="anonymous"` from a host that sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`. See [Tracker → What counts as an error](/en/tracker#what-counts). Where `ScriptError` is present, the error-types response lists `own_subdomain_hosts`, usually where the header is missing.

How much detail you see depends on the site's [tracker settings](/en/tracker): error **type**, **count**, and [**message shape**](/en/tracker#message-shape) arrive always. The error **message** needs [`collect_error_messages`](/en/tracker#collect-error-messages), and full **stack frames** need [`collect_error_frames`](/en/tracker#collect-error-frames).

### Failed resource loads [#failed-resources]

A failed `<img>`, `<script>`, or `<link>` is its own signal, with its own tab, its own rate (`failed_resource_rate`), and its own curve beside the error rate. It is split into two buckets:

- **Your own hosts**: the actionable half. A broken image path, a bundle that 404s after a deploy.
- **Foreign hosts**: mostly ad blockers and tracking protection.

Ownership is the registrable-domain rule above. **The list groups by host**, each with its share of all failed loads, and opening a host shows the individual resources under it. A second view ranks pages by the share of their pageviews that lost a resource.

**Hiding a host** narrows the totals too: `host` and `exclude_hosts` do, `ownership` does not.

**Opening a resource drills into it** the way opening an error signature does: the file, a time series, breakdowns over page, browser, device, OS, and country, the visitors affected, and sample beacons. Pass the row's `fingerprint` to [`POST /analytics/failed-resources/detail`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsFailedResourceDetail). The error drill-down refuses a resource key.

`Failed resources` is a filter and a metric of its own, so `>= 1` scopes any view to pageviews where something did not load. In the pageview drill-down the failed loads have their own block, separate from the JavaScript errors.

The path needs [`collect_error_frames`](/en/tracker#collect-error-frames).

Ad-blocked requests to known tracker hosts count as `blocked_count`, a subset of the foreign bucket and not failures.

The list endpoint is [`POST /analytics/failed-resources`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsFailedResources). The error-types endpoint refuses `error_type: ["ResourceLoadError"]` with a 400 pointing here.

## Visitors [#visitors]

The Visitors page lists distinct visitors with start time, duration, page count, country, device, average LCP, and errors. Three columns read the visit at a glance:

- **Outcome**: ordered, left the checkout, cart, browsed, or bounced.
- **Source**: where the visitor came in, such as Google search, a newsletter, or direct.
- **Journey**: one block per page, its width the time spent there and its colour the page type. A red mark flags errors, a check an order.

The summary above the list covers the whole period: visitors, pageviews per visitor, and the share with a JavaScript error, slow visitors (average LCP over 4 s), and buyers. **Segment chips** (with errors, slow, ordered, reached checkout, cart activity, one page only) set the behavior filters, and each shows how many visitors it lists. "Adjust behavior" opens every filter: pages, errors, duration, LCP, page type, and funnel stage. Orders, checkout, and cart show only on a shop.

**A visitor opens in a dialog** wherever it is linked, and `?visitor=` links to it.

- **The journey** shows every pageview in order with Web Vitals, errors, page type, and on shops the [checkout stage](/en/checkout-funnel) and cart actions. Checkout events and [NEL](/en/nel#dashboard) and [CSP reports](/en/csp#dashboard) sit between them, abandoned loads only once you switch them on.
- **The entry and every return from outside** show on the timeline and before that page, with any campaign. See [`arrival`](/en/concepts/sessions#arrival).
- **Without pageviews** in the period, it lists the visitor's reports and events alone.
- **A click on a page** opens its full detail, and "Open in Explorer" lists all of the visitor's pageviews there.
- **Refresh** picks up new pages of a visit still going on. The live switch does it every 7 seconds.

A visitor is counted by [`stitch`](/en/tracker#store-stitch) or `session`, as `count_visitors_by` sets. The `Minimal` preset stores neither and leaves the list empty. See [Visitors and sessions](/en/concepts/sessions).

## Fetch/XHR and Server-Timing

The Fetch/XHR view has an aggregate waterfall per endpoint and a per-beacon detail. The Server-Timing page is described under [Server-Timing → In the dashboard](/en/guides/server-timing#dashboard).

The response breakdown of one pageview groups its rows by who can act on them: network and visitor, server, browser. The [redirects](/en/web-vitals/ttfb#redirects) before the fetch are their own row, and a missing value says why it is missing.

An expanded endpoint shows its own p75 or p95 over time with its calls and release markers, and stays in the URL. A page with more than 24 API endpoints keeps 24 of them named, and the rest sum into a nameless `other` row. Ad-blocked calls in that row do not count as failures.

The Fetch/XHR error rate counts failures on your own hosts, by the same registrable-domain rule as the [error rate](#failed-resources). Failures on foreign hosts are reported beside it as their own number, mostly the visitor's ad blocker. The panel's 1P/3P filter follows the same rule.

## Experience Score

The [Experience Score](/en/web-vitals/experience-score) panel breaks the score down by segment (all visitors, desktop, mobile, and slow connections), each with its grade, numeric score, and the per-metric values behind it.

## API [#api]

Every panel maps to an endpoint under `/organizations/{org_id}/analytics/…`, most of them to the [query endpoint](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalytics).

| Field | What it does |
| ----- | ------------ |
| `granularity` | `minute`, `five_minutes`, `fifteen_minutes`, `hour`, `six_hours`, `day`, `week`, `month`, or `auto` by the [rule above](#resolution). Widths under an hour allow at most 1500 buckets, a longer request is a 422. |
| `timezone` | An IANA name such as `Europe/Berlin`. Buckets of 6 hours and longer start at its boundaries, timestamps stay UTC. Weeks start on Monday either way. |
| `meta.granularity_options` | The widths with 4 to 1500 buckets over the window. |
| `sample_count` | The pageviews behind a row, unweighted under sampling. `beacons` is its deprecated twin. |
| `url_path`, `url_key`, `url_key_set` | Group or filter by the URL without its query, by each query key, or by the set of keys. A pageview counts once per key, and `meta.pageviews` is the denominator. |
| `url_param` + `url_param_key` | Group by the value of one query key. A key the application does not store answers 422 naming `collection.query_values_for`. |
| `""` as a text filter | Selects the rows without a value: `referrer_source: ""` answers direct visits. `exclude: {utm_campaign: [""]}` keeps the pageviews with a campaign. |
| `filters.hour_of_day` | `{from, to}` keeps those local hours, both included, and `{from: 22, to: 6}` wraps midnight. It needs `timezone`, else 422. The query, metric summary, panels, visitor, event, checkout, NEL, and CSP routes take it, every other route answers 422. |
| `sparse_buckets` | `{min_pageviews, exclude}` with the threshold from `POST /analytics/sparse-hours`. `exclude: true` drops those hours, and `meta.sparse_buckets.excluded_hour_count` counts them. Rows below the threshold carry `is_sparse`. The `{mode, min_pageviews}` form is deprecated. |
| `POST /analytics/sparse-hours` | The thin complete hours of a window and their threshold. `share` sets the thinnest part, 0.2 by default, with at least 30 measurements per hour. |
| `time_range` | At most the organization's history, 90 days or 13 months with the history feature. A longer window is a 422 `time_range_beyond_history` with the horizon in `details.history_days`. `history_days` on the organization and `data_starts_at` on organizations, applications, and sites bound it. |

Only a window of at most 6 hours that ends within 10 minutes of now reads live beacons. Every other window reads the finalized pageviews.
