Analytics is the exploration layer on top of everything your beacons collect: who is on the site right now, where traffic comes from, which pages get viewed, which errors fire, and how whole visits unfold.

## The dashboard

The main Analytics view for a site shows:

- **Current visitors**: unique visitors in the last 5 minutes, expandable to a per-minute chart of the last 15 plus the pages being viewed right now and the sources they came through. On shops the card also shows how many of them are at the cart, checkout, and success stage.
- **Headline metrics**: Visitors, Pageviews, Pageviews per visitor, and Checkouts, each with a sparkline and the change against the previous period. The count tiles also show an average per minute over the selected range, so a day and a month compare on the same footing. Clicking a tile explains what it counts.
- **Trend chart**: the selected headline metric over the range, with an optional previous-period overlay. The selection lives in the URL (`?metric=`), so a shared link opens on the same metric. It always starts as a line chart, whatever the period. The legend sits above it with the bar/area switch, and each series can be switched off on its own and carries its own previous-period value.
- **Breakdowns**: four panels for Sources, Pages, Locations, and Devices, each with sub-tabs for drilling down (channel vs. UTM source, country lists, browser vs. OS, and so on). The Pages panel's third tab lists the **domains** out of the page URLs. Each panel says what it counts, reports in its footer how many values sit behind the eight rows shown, and leads into the [Explorer](#explorer) through "Show all". A single row opens there too.
- **Breakdown grid**: on a metric's own page every dimension is a card, all of them visible at once, each with a share link that reopens it. Every bar is grey and says how much, the number beside it carries the colour, and each list says how much of the answer it is showing before leading into the Explorer with the page's filters applied.
- **Checkout funnel**: one bar per stage on a shared edge, with the share of the stage before it, so where people drop out is visible. Underneath, cart requests by kind. See [Checkout funnel](/en/checkout-funnel).
- **Traffic quality**: the split from bot classification, first as one bar, then as clickable rows.
- **Active hours**: hour of day by weekday, so it is visible when something actually happens on the site. The grid only shows the weekdays the selected window reached.
- **Engagement**: the share of visitors who saw a single page, with the distribution of pages per visitor and its median underneath. Not a bounce rate: whether somebody left afterwards is not in the data.

Visitor counts are anonymous by construction; see [Privacy](/en/privacy) for what a "visitor" can and cannot mean here.

## Filtering

A filter bar sits next to the time picker on the dashboard, the overview, and every metric detail page. Type a dimension and value (for example `country = DE`) and press Enter to add a filter chip; the value field autocompletes with the real values for the current time range and shows how many pageviews each one has. The dimension picker lists the most-used dimensions first (Page, Country, Device), then the rest: browser, OS, connection, viewport, referrer source and medium, UTM fields, click-ID label, domain, navigation type, protocol, and the [checkout stage](/en/checkout-funnel) (cart, checkout, completed).

Filters live in the URL, so they survive a reload, are shareable, and carry across navigation between views. Multiple chips combine with AND; a comma inside one value means "any of". Click a chip to edit it; its X removes it. The "Filter" button carries a badge with the active count.

**Every page starts without bot traffic**, so the numbers describe real visitors unless you say otherwise. The filter menu's Traffic control switches between No bots, Bots only, and All, and the page header says which is in force next to the period. The scope is not counted as a filter and survives "Clear all", because it is what a page counts rather than something you narrowed down.

Every chip also has an on/off switch. Switched off, the chip keeps its values and stays in place, drawn struck through, and the query runs without it. A switched-off filter narrows nothing and is not counted in the badge, and clicking the same country on the map switches it back on instead of adding a second one. The state travels in the URL, so a shared link opens with the same filters on and off.

## The Explorer [#explorer]

The Explorer groups pageviews by any dimension and charts the metrics, or shows the raw rows. Maximize gives it the whole window, sidebar included, for as long as you are on the page. "Show all" and the per-row links from the dashboard panels carry the page's filters into it, including exclusions, metric ranges, and the bot switches, and it opens with the numbers the page is about (pageviews and visitors, plus LCP p75 on pages). Clicking a row adds its value to the filters rather than replacing them, so drilling in from a page filtered to Germany narrows instead of emptying the table.

An **Events** page sits beside the table, one row per event the tracker reported, filterable by type and opening the visitor a row belongs to. It appears for an organization whose applications can produce events, which today means a Shopify shop and its [checkout events](/en/checkout-funnel#shopify).

Results can be copied as Markdown or downloaded as CSV: same rows in the same order, values unformatted, the unit in the header, so a spreadsheet can compute with them.

## Navigating with the command palette [#command-palette]

Press `Cmd`/`Ctrl`+`K` (or the command button in the top bar) to open a searchable command menu that drives the whole app: jump to any page, switch organization or site, change the time range and granularity, and add, remove, or clear filters. Start typing to search everything at once, or drill into a category with `Enter` or `→`; `←` or `Backspace` steps back and `Esc` closes.

Typing searches **across every organization you can reach**: your own memberships, the client organizations of any agency you belong to, and the applications and domains inside them. Each hit says which organization it lives in. Applications lead the results, and picking one sets the application filter on the page you are on. Inactive sites and applications are listed and flagged.

Every page is listed with the group it belongs to, so typing a group ("network") lists everything under it, and a page is also found by what it is called elsewhere ("API" finds Fetch/XHR). Opening an organization lists its applications above its domains.

The same search is available from a script as [`GET /search`](/en/api/search/search).

## Error Analytics [#error-analytics]

The Errors view has two tabs, JavaScript errors and [failed resources](#failed-resources), each with its own headline, chart, and list. On the errors tab:

- **Error rate over time**: the share of pageviews with at least one JavaScript error. A failed image, script, or stylesheet runs as its own curve on the same chart.
- **Which errors, over time**: the five biggest signatures per time bucket as a stacked chart, named by what broke rather than by their hash. Clicking a name in the legend opens that signature in the list below.
- **The list is one row per fault.** Browsers report the same throw site a few characters apart, and a second caller of the same function is a second stack, so a row counts every variant and says how many signatures it stands for. On the API this is `group_variants` on `POST /analytics/error-types`.
- **A row leads with what broke, in a sentence**: what happened, whose code it is, the file it was thrown from, how often it occurred and when it last did. Each row carries a spark line and a "New" or "Rising" badge, and the list sorts by most frequent, new, rising, or last seen.
- **Ranked by the pageviews an error reached**, not by how often it fired. A single visit stuck in a loop is marked as a loop instead of topping the list, and "Most frequent" is still an ordering you can pick.
- **Every error has its own page** at `/org/<org>/errors/<fingerprint>`, which is also its share link. Existing `?error=` links redirect there.
- **Pages with the most errors**: ranked by how many errors a page threw, not by its error rate. A page with one pageview that threw once has a rate of 100 % and is not the broken checkout.
- **Error origin**: how pageviews with an error split between your own hosts and foreign ones. The third bar holds pageviews without an error plus errors with no host to attribute them to.

### What broke, and where [#error-detail]

**An error says what broke in a sentence**, even where the site keeps no error messages. The tracker recognizes the browser's wording and sends its [shape](/en/tracker#message-shape) rather than the text, so a row reads "`uc.setServiceAlias` was called, but `uc` does not exist at this point" instead of showing a stack alone.

**"Resolve this stack" reads the minified file your visitors actually ran.** Each frame comes back with the method it is in, the enclosing module, and the code at that column, syntax-colored and scrolled to the failing call. No source map and no setup on your side: a minifier keeps class and method names. It is a button rather than something a row does by itself, because it fetches your bundle. The snippet is laid out in lines with the throwing line marked, and only whitespace is inserted, so the reported column still points at its code.

- A run of frames in the same vendor bundle folds into one row naming the library. When every frame is a library, the panel says so: the call that made the mistake was cut off at five frames.
- A frame that only passes the error on is marked as a rethrow, and the cause sits further down. The top frame of a minified stack is regularly a rethrow inside a promise polyfill.
- A frame that cannot be read says why: no path was stored, the file sits on somebody else's host, the file is the fastmon tracker, or you deployed again under the same name and the position is gone.
- The endpoint is [`POST /analytics/error-types/symbolicate`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsErrorSymbolication). The same panel sits in the Explorer's row drawer: open a pageview, expand its errors, and the stack resolves there too.

**"Where it happens" is the breakdown grid the metric pages carry**: page, page type, domain, country, device, browser, OS, connection, navigation, caches, bots, and network. Bots and network are among them, so you can see whether an error reaches real visitors or only crawlers.

**An error with nothing in it says why.** A signature can arrive with a five-figure count, no message, and no stack: messages or frames switched off for this application, a third-party script served without the CORS opt-in, or collection on and nothing sent anyway. The chart and the breakdown work either way.

**"AI prompt" hands the whole case to an assistant**: what broke, the numbers, the resolved stack with the code at the throw site, where it happens, and sample occurrences. It also says what your site does not collect, so nothing reads as a zero that is really a setting.

**Which API calls fail alongside it.** [`POST /analytics/error-types/correlated-requests`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsErrorCorrelatedRequests) ranks the fetch/XHR endpoints that fail in the same pageviews, each against its failure share in pageviews without that error. Built for the rejection with no message and no stack, and it says endpoints occur together, never that one caused the other.

**An error signature is a filter like any other.** Every error row links into the Explorer scoped to the pageviews that carried it, and "Error signature" is in the filter list, so LCP, browsers, countries, or the checkout funnel can be read over the traffic where one thing breaks. `filters.error_fingerprint_kind` says which list the key came from (`error` or `failed_resource`). A query using it reads the raw rows rather than a rollup, and costs accordingly.

**Your own subdomains count as yours.** First party means the same registrable domain as the page, in the error rate, the error origin, the Fetch/XHR split, and the resource list alike.

`ScriptError` is an uncaught exception in a script from another origin loaded without CORS opt-in, so the browser hides the filename, line, and message. Load the script with `crossorigin="anonymous"` from a host that sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` and the browser reports the real error. See [Tracker → What counts as an error](/en/tracker#what-counts). Where `ScriptError` is present, the error-types response lists `own_subdomain_hosts`, usually where the header is missing.

How much detail you see depends on the site's [tracker settings](/en/tracker): error **type**, **count**, and [**message shape**](/en/tracker#message-shape) arrive always. The error **message** needs [`collect_error_messages`](/en/tracker#collect-error-messages), and full **stack frames** need [`collect_error_frames`](/en/tracker#collect-error-frames).

### Failed resource loads [#failed-resources]

A failed `<img>`, `<script>`, or `<link>` is its own signal, with its own tab, its own rate (`failed_resource_rate`), and its own curve beside the error rate. It is split into two buckets:

- **Your own hosts**: the actionable half. A broken image path, a bundle that 404s after a deploy.
- **Foreign hosts**: dominated by ad blockers and tracking protection, so not a defect of the site.

Ownership is the registrable-domain rule above. **The list groups by host**, each with its share of all failed loads, and opening a host shows the individual resources under it. A second view ranks pages by the share of their pageviews that lost a resource.

**Hide a host and the list becomes readable.** `host` and `exclude_hosts` narrow the totals as well, unlike `ownership`, so the share bars and the big number keep agreeing with the rows.

**Opening a resource drills into it** the way opening an error signature does: the file, a time series, breakdowns over page, browser, device, OS, and country, the visitors affected, and sample beacons. Pass the row's `fingerprint` to [`POST /analytics/failed-resources/detail`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsFailedResourceDetail). The error drill-down refuses a resource key.

`Failed resources` is a filter and a metric of its own, so `>= 1` scopes any view to pageviews where something did not load. In the pageview drill-down the failed loads have their own block, separate from the JavaScript errors.

The path is often empty, because it needs [`collect_error_frames`](/en/tracker#collect-error-frames). A failed load can also be a shaky connection or a request the visitor interrupted, not necessarily something wrong on your server.

Ad-blocked requests to known tracker hosts stay visible as a subset of the foreign bucket, reported as `blocked_count`: read it as "N excluded as ad-blocked", not as a failure.

The list endpoint is [`POST /analytics/failed-resources`](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsFailedResources). The error-types endpoint refuses `error_type: ["ResourceLoadError"]` with a 400 pointing here.

## Visitors

The Visitors explorer lists distinct visitors with start and end time, duration, page count, country, device, average LCP and FCP, and error count. A per-visitor detail view shows every pageview in order, each with its Web Vitals, errors, page type, and on shops its [checkout stage](/en/checkout-funnel) and cart actions.

The summary above the list describes the whole selected period, not the loaded page of results: visitors, visitors with a single page, and the median pages per visitor. Behavior filters narrow the list to visitors with at least a given number of pages, errors, duration, or LCP, with a given page type in the journey, or who reached a funnel stage. They stay on the Visitors and Sessions pages and are dropped when you navigate elsewhere.

A visitor can be deep-linked with `?visitor=`, and the journey header then says who the visitor was. In the list the full ID is click-to-copy on hover, and a row action narrows the page to that visitor.

A visitor is counted along an identity axis: [`stitch`](/en/tracker#store-stitch) or `session`, and the site's `count_visitors_by` sets the default. New sites start on `Standard`, which stores `stitch`; the `Minimal` preset stores neither and leaves the list empty. The per-visitor replay needs the `session` identity. See [Visitors and sessions](/en/concepts/sessions).

## Fetch/XHR and Server-Timing

The Fetch/XHR view has an aggregate waterfall (per-endpoint start offsets, duration histograms and percentiles, a status-error distribution) plus a per-beacon detail. The Server-Timing view breaks out the metrics your backend reports. Both read fields the tracker adds to each beacon (see [the beacon](/en/concepts/beacon)).

A page with more than 24 API endpoints keeps 24 of them named, and the rest sum into a nameless `other` row. Ad-blocked calls in that row do not count as failures.

The Fetch/XHR error rate counts failures on your own hosts, by the same registrable-domain rule as the [error rate](#failed-resources). Failures on foreign hosts are reported beside it as their own number, mostly the visitor's ad blocker. The panel's 1P/3P filter follows the same rule.

The beacon reads the header on every pageview, and the dashboards on top of it are still being tuned. To emit the header and learn how fastmon splits it into backend phases, cache status, and custom keys, see [Server-Timing headers](/en/guides/server-timing).

## Experience Score

The [Experience Score](/en/web-vitals/experience-score) panel breaks the score down by segment (all visitors, desktop, mobile, and slow connections), each with its grade, numeric score, and the per-metric values behind it. Useful for the classic case where the overall grade looks fine but mobile-on-3G is failing.

## API

Every panel maps to a query endpoint under `/organizations/{org_id}/analytics/…`: a flexible [query endpoint](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalytics) for time series and breakdowns, plus dedicated endpoints for histograms, error types and details, visitors, Fetch/XHR, Server-Timing, resource and third-party breakdowns, LoAF, the [checkout funnel](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsCheckoutFunnel), and the Experience Score. See the [Analytics endpoint reference](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalytics).
