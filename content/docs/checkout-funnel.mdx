The checkout funnel counts how far visitors get through your shop. fastmon marks every pageview whose URL is a cart, checkout, or success page, records the cart requests your pages send, and counts per visitor which stages they reached.

<Callout type="info" title="Facts only">
  Every number is a count of something observed: a visitor loaded a cart page, a visitor loaded a thank-you page. There is no "abandoned" metric, because that would be a claim built from a missing observation. The visitor may have bought on another device, after the daily `stitch` rotation, or on a thank-you page without the tracker.
</Callout>

## How a pageview gets its stage

At ingest the collector matches the sanitized page URL (origin and path, plus any query keys you opted into) against a list of patterns per stage and stamps `cart`, `checkout`, or `success` on the pageview. Pageviews that match nothing keep an empty stage. The tracker sends no extra field.

The built-in patterns cover Shopware 5 and 6, WooCommerce (English and German slugs), Magento 2, PrestaShop, JTL Shop 5, and Shopify. Every pattern tolerates a language or shop prefix, so `/de/warenkorb` and `/fr/cart` count like their root variants.

Matching order is fixed: `success`, then `cart`, then `checkout`. Success pages often live under the checkout path (`/checkout/finish`), and Shopware and Magento put the cart there too.

The marks are stored with the pageview and never recomputed. Changing the patterns affects pageviews from that moment on.

### Default patterns [#default-patterns]

A pattern must describe the whole path. `*` matches any run of characters, including `/`; everything else is literal. Matching is case-insensitive.

| Stage        | Built-in patterns | Shop systems |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ------------ |
| **cart**     | `*/checkout/cart*`, `*/cart`, `*/cart/*`, `*/warenkorb`, `*/warenkorb/*`, `*/warenkorb.*` | Shopware, Magento 2, WooCommerce, Shopify, PrestaShop, JTL 5 |
| **checkout** | `*/checkout`, `*/checkout/*`, `*/kasse`, `*/kasse/*`, `*/bestellvorgang`, `*/bestellvorgang/*`, `*/bestellvorgang.*`, `*/bestellung`, `*/bestellung/*`, `/order` | Shopware, Magento 2, WooCommerce, JTL 5, PrestaShop, custom German shops |
| **success**  | `*/checkout/finish*`, `*/checkout/onepage/success*`, `*/order-received/*`, `*/order-confirmation`, `*/order-confirmation/*`, `*/bestellabschluss`, `*/bestellabschluss/*`, `*/bestellabschluss.*`, `*/checkout/success*`, `*/thankyou`, `*/thankyou/*`, `*/thank-you`, `*/thank-you/*`, `*/danke`, `*/danke/*`, `*/danke.*` | Shopware, Magento 2, WooCommerce, PrestaShop, JTL 5, generic thank-you pages |

The patterns are word-bounded: `*/cart` matches the cart page but not `/cartoons`, and `*/thank-you` does not match `/thank-you-for-subscribing`. `/order` is exact and root-only, or it would match Shopware's `/account/order`.

The switch runs on every site, shops or not. On a site with paths like `/docs/checkout/setup`, those pageviews pick up a stage; the remedy is custom patterns or the opt-out. OXID needs the [query recipe](#oxid). For Shopify, see [Shopify checkout events](#shopify).

## Cart interactions [#cart-interactions]

The URL stages see pages. A visitor who adds a product from a listing or a drawer cart never loads a cart page, so the funnel also records **cart interactions**: a first-party `POST` to a known cart endpoint, read from the fetch/XHR telemetry the tracker already collects. Where the endpoint reveals it, the interaction carries a kind.

| Kind       | Endpoints (POST) | Shop systems |
| ---------- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| **add**    | `/checkout/line-item/add`, `/checkout/ajaxaddarticlecart`, `/checkout/cart/add/*`, `*/cart/add`, `*/cart/add.js`, `/wp-json/wc/store/v1/cart/add-item` | Shopware 6, Shopware 5, Magento 2, Shopify, WooCommerce (Store API) |
| **change** | `/checkout/line-item/update`, `/checkout/line-item/change-quantity/*`, `*/cart/change`, `*/cart/change.js`, `*/cart/update`, `*/cart/update.js`, `/wp-json/wc/store/v1/cart/update-item` | Shopware 6, Shopify, WooCommerce (Store API) |
| **remove** | `/checkout/line-item/delete/*`, `/wp-json/wc/store/v1/cart/remove-item` | Shopware 6, WooCommerce (Store API) |
| **other**  | `/cart` | PrestaShop, Shopify Hydrogen |

On Shopify a quantity of 0 is a remove and arrives as `change`. The kind of an `other` row lives in the request body, which fastmon never reads.

Cart interactions need [`collect_fetch_xhr`](/en/tracker#collect-fetch-xhr) (on under Standard). With it off, the URL stages still work and the cart counts stay empty. Adds that go out as a classic form POST (OXID, WooCommerce single-product pages) are invisible to fetch/XHR. The endpoint list is maintained server-side and grows without a tracker release.

Cart interactions are reported **beside** the funnel steps, never as one: their coverage depends on the shop system and on the fetch/XHR setting.

## What the numbers mean [#what-the-numbers-mean]

- **Conversion rate**: visitors with a success over all visitors in the window, the number GA4 and Shopify report. Hold it against the usual 1 to 3 % shop benchmark. `visitors_total` is the denominator, and each point of the series carries its own `visitors`.
- **`reach_rate` per step**: the share of the first step. It can exceed 100 %, because a visitor can enter a later step without the earlier one being observed (a deep link, a drawer cart, an express checkout).
- **Visitors per step.** Each visitor counts once per step they reached in the window, grouped on the visitor identity (`stitch` by default, see [Visitors and sessions](/en/concepts/sessions)). Nothing per visitor is stored; the grouping happens at query time.
- **Bucketed by entry.** In the series a visitor lands in the bucket of their first funnel row, not of their success. Bucket visitors do not add up to the window total. A day is final once the day, the 24-hour `stitch` window, and the ingest lag have passed.
- **Add-to-cart rate** (`cart_interaction_rate`): visitors who used the cart over all visitors, typically 5 to 6 %. Read it as a floor wherever the shop's cart calls are invisible to fetch/XHR.
- **Visitors whose cart calls failed** (`cart_interaction_error_visitors`): against `cart_interactions`, the share of shoppers who hit a broken cart. It moves before the conversion rate does.
- **Time to purchase** (`time_to_success_seconds`): seconds from funnel entry to the first success, as p50, p75 and p90. A p90 pulling away from the p50 is the checkout getting in the way.
- **Success is a page, not a payment** (outside Shopify). A shop whose thank-you page does not load the tracker shows checkouts without successes. That is the most common setup issue.
- **A rate that could not be measured is absent.** If the visitor count times out, the response keeps its steps and omits `visitors_total` and `conversion_rate` rather than reporting 0 %.

Filters that pick funnel pages (`checkout_stage`, the cart interaction thresholds) narrow the steps but not the denominator. Audience filters (site, country, device, campaign) narrow both halves.

## Shopify checkout events [#shopify]

Shopify runs its checkout on its own infrastructure, where your theme script never loads, so the URL stages end at the cart. A custom pixel reports three checkout steps as events: checkout entered, payment submitted, order placed. Setup, and what limits the coverage, is in [Track the Shopify checkout](/en/guides/shopify-checkout#numbers).

What changes in the funnel once the pixel runs:

- **`checkout` and `success` come from events**, with no pageview on either. Visitors seen only in the checkout join `visitors_total`.
- **`payment_submitted_visitors`** is a fact beside the steps: visitors who submitted payment. It is absent, not 0, when no event was read for the window.
- **Purchases are a lower bound.** Shopify does not fire its checkout completed event for most Shop Pay and Apple Pay orders. A visitor with a payment and no success most likely bought through a wallet.
- **Content blockers stop the pixel.** EasyList's EasyPrivacy list blocks Shopify's custom-pixel sandbox path, and uBlock Origin ships that list by default. Those visitors contribute storefront pageviews but no checkout events, so the conversion rate reads low. See [content blockers](/en/guides/shopify-checkout#blockers).
- **Cart to checkout can exceed 100 %.** A buyer who went straight into the checkout never loaded a cart page.
- **Events need the edge identifier** ([`store_stitch`](/en/tracker#store-stitch)). A filter the event rows cannot answer (URL, page type, campaign, a metric threshold) leaves them out of that request.
- **Cohorts and journeys stay on pageviews.** A buyer whose whole run is checkout events appears in the success step and in no cohort, and the sessions explorer does not show the Shopify checkout.

The events carry the step, the visitor's country, device, browser and traffic class, and the edge identifier. No URL, no session id, no order value, and no Web Vitals: the Shopify checkout is counted, not measured.

## Where it shows up

- **Analytics dashboard.** Checkouts is the fourth headline metric, with its own tile and sparkline. Selecting it draws the success series with cart and checkout as context lines. The checkout panel lists the steps and the cart requests by kind.
- **Stage chart.** Visitors per stage over time, counted when they reached it and including a Shopify pixel's checkout events, with the previous period compared the same way for all three.
- **`checkout_stage` filter.** Cart, checkout, and completed are fixed options in the filter bar, the chips, and the command palette. On the metric pages Web Vitals per funnel stage is a filtered query.
- **Explorer.** Group by Checkout Stage, chart Cart Actions and Failed Cart Actions, and read stage and kinds as columns. A "Checkout" preset brings stages over time and LCP per stage.
- **Visitors.** Filter the list to everyone who reached a stage or issued a cart interaction. On the journey each pageview carries a Funnel chip colored by stage.
- **Business Impact.** Where the funnel has data, the page shows the measured conversion rate instead of the estimate derived from LCP and INP.

## Settings

The switch and the patterns live on the application under **Settings → Checkout funnel**, and on the API under `shop.checkout` (see [Applications](/en/concepts/applications#checkout-funnel)).

- **`checkout_tracking`** is on by default and is the opt-out. It covers the URL stages and the cart interactions together. Marking is forward-only, so turning it off leaves a gap you cannot fill later.
- **Per-stage paths.** Each stage either inherits the built-in patterns (the default, `null`) or replaces them wholesale. There is no merge. Up to 10 patterns per stage, each up to 200 characters, lowercase printable ASCII, `*` the only wildcard. Improvements to the built-in list reach every site that did not override that stage.
- **Requirements.** Counting visitors per stage needs a stored visitor identity ([`store_stitch`](/en/tracker#store-stitch), or sessions); without one the funnel endpoints answer `422`. Cart interactions additionally need [`collect_fetch_xhr`](/en/tracker#collect-fetch-xhr).

### Query-based checkouts (OXID and others) [#oxid]

OXID keeps the whole checkout on `index.php` and distinguishes the steps only in the `cl` query parameter. Add `cl` to [`collect_query_values_for`](/en/tracker#collect-query-values-for), then set custom patterns: cart `*?cl=basket*`, checkout `*?cl=user*`, `*?cl=payment*`, `*?cl=order*`, success `*?cl=thankyou*`. If other query keys can precede `cl`, add the `*&cl=…*` variants. The same recipe covers generic `?step=` checkouts. Query patterns are not part of the defaults.

## Privacy

The stage is a four-value label computed from the URL the site already stores; the cart interaction is a flag computed from fetch/XHR paths it already stores. Neither adds a field to the beacon or visitor entropy. Nothing is kept per visitor, and the funnel is computed at query time in every collection mode, including `anonymous`.

The Shopify pixel is the one part that sends its own events. What it carries is listed under [Shopify checkout events](#shopify). See [Privacy](/en/privacy#what-we-collect).

## API

- `POST /organizations/{org_id}/analytics/funnels/checkout`: the steps with visitor counts and `reach_rate`, the window `conversion_rate` and `visitors_total`, the cart interaction counts by kind, `time_to_success_seconds`, and the series bucketed by funnel entry. See the [reference](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsCheckoutFunnel).
- `POST /organizations/{org_id}/analytics/funnels/checkout/cohorts`: visitors with an observed success against those who reached the checkout without one. Per group, the Web Vitals over the whole journey and per stage, plus how many cart calls went out, how slow they were and how many failed. Both groups need a stored visitor identity, or the endpoint answers `422`. See the [reference](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsCheckoutCohorts).
- `POST /organizations/{org_id}/analytics/funnels/checkout/stages`: the visitors who reached each stage per hour or day, checkout events included, for the stage chart. `compare_to` adds the previous period, and it reaches back only as far as a Shopify pixel has been installed, so an earlier comparison shows the installation as a rise. `events_read` says whether the events were counted.
- `POST /organizations/{org_id}/analytics/funnels/checkout/journeys`: the pageviews around a visitor's hit on a given step, with the visitor's checkout `events` beside `pages`. Visitors are selected from both sources, so one known only from events comes back with an empty `pages`. See the [reference](/en/api/analytics/queryAnalyticsCheckoutJourneys).
- `POST /organizations/{org_id}/analytics/visitors` accepts `contains_checkout_stage` and `contains_cart_interaction` as filters, and a checkout event counts as reaching its stage. Each row is a pageview row, so a visitor known only from events has none. The visitor detail answers `events` beside `pages`, each with the name, the stage, the source and the time.
- `POST /organizations/{org_id}/analytics/events` lists the checkout events themselves, newest first and cursor paginated, so a test order can be held against the Shopify order list. Narrow it with `names`, the site scope, `filters.stitch` for one visitor, and the edge-derived dimensions.
- `checkout_stage` is a filter and exclude dimension on every analytics endpoint that takes `filters` (`exclude: {checkout_stage: ['']}` keeps only funnel pageviews), and a group-by dimension in `POST /analytics/query`. `cart_interactions` and `cart_interaction_errors` are metrics there (and therefore usable in notification rules) and metric ranges. `POST /analytics/beacons` projects `checkout_stage`, the two counters, and the `cart_interaction_kinds` map onto each row.
